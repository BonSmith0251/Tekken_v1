records. Source bytes, offsets and hashes are retained in
Artifacts/CameraEffects/OriginalExport (workspace root) and the installed
Resources/CameraEffects/source-manifest.json. There are additional camera layouts
outside this look-at family; these are not exported as T7CF by this tool.

3 through 6. BattleCameraEffects shares these bindings with VersusGame and the
demo. Shake amplitude/waveform remain the existing remaster interpretation.

payload length and finite samples. The refreshed original camera tracks are used
by the existing versus cinematic path. No new guessed automatic camera trigger
has been added. KO/airborne zoom and original side/timing/attachment semantics
remain subject to further original-game comparison. Visual parity is not claimed.

CAMERA SHAKE, RECOVERED FROM THE EXECUTABLE (27 Oct 2026)
The shake is no longer a remaster guess. Tools/CameraEffects/export_shake_presets.py reads the
inspected executable (SHA-256 f46f9ff5...) and writes the ten original presets into
Resources/CameraEffects/catalog.json (shakePresets, shakeUnitRadians, shakeEvidence):
  setter 0x1406FF050 stores the preset in three camera objects and resets their frame;
  camB update 0x1406EDCE0 (vtable 0x1428BBFA0 slot 1) calls the evaluator each 60 Hz battle tick,
  advances the frame, and clears the preset when the evaluator returns -1;
  evaluator 0x140701FD0 picks (table, length) by preset (jump table 0x1407024C0) and, for frame
  f < length-1, adds values[f] * 2*pi/65536 rad to the eye->target PITCH (positive looks up).
  No lateral or positional motion.
  Presets (frames, peak): 1 (24, 0.70 deg)  2 (6, 0.70)  3 (12, 0.70)  4 (18, 1.05)  5 (24, 1.05)
  6 (42, 2.11)  7 (42, 3.16)  8 (24, 0.18)  9 (24, 0.13)  10 (6, 0.35); all decaying 6-frame (10 Hz) nods.
Dispatch from move data: 0x8001-0x8004 and 0x8006-0x8009 -> presets 3-6; 0x8005 and 0x800A pass
the property value (min(value, 9); 0 stops the shake). These sit on landing (dwup_*, aDw_tataki,
Fall_*), bound, screw, wall-hit (wDm_*) and Rage Art finish moves, not on ordinary hits.
Versus: VersusGame.OnMoveProperty starts the preset (a new one replaces the running one at frame 0),
AfterTick advances it per battle tick (so KO slow motion slows it), and the camera pitches about its
right axis by BattleCameraEffects.OriginalShakePitch (Rage Art camera included). Removed as having
no basis in the data: the per-hit shake (shake = .4 / .12 on guard) and the extra KO Impact(.06).
The camera demo shows the original tables. CPU matches (harness ShakeEvents): 0-3 shakes per round.
Not wired (callers found, event identity unverified): native requests for preset 6 (0x1406DEF4B,
after a camera mode 7 setup), preset 4 (0x140711298, after common effects 0 and 7) and preset 3
(0x140714B78; 0x1407911C2 after common effect 14). The reference video's large KO nod has the same
6-frame cycle and a size close to preset 7; which native path plays it at a KO is not established.

WHY THE CAMERA RARELY SHAKES (investigated 27 Oct 2026, after "not at all")
Ordinary knockdowns carry no shake property in the original data: sDw_UP_01_, sDw_AIR00_,
sDm_ROL00*, sDw_KAO00_, sDm_PNG00_ and the dwDWN_*/dwKAM_* loops after them have none. Only spins,
screws, bounds, wall hits and Rage Art finishes do, so CPU matches see 0-3 property shakes a round
(harness ShakeReach / ShakeEvents). The four native requests, traced in the executable:
  0x140714B78 in 0x140713DD0: on an event that flags BOTH fighters (+0xD7D, +0x754 |= 0x100) and
    starts a slowdown (0x140740A30 with globals 10 / 24), shake preset = 2 + level, level a static
    global (0x1434DF5DC = 3 -> preset 5; the other three globals 0x1434DF5D0/D4/D8 = 10/24/17,
    written only by the static initializer 0x140701A65). A both-fighter slow-motion event, not
    ordinary hits.
  0x140711298 (preset 4), called from the same function, after common effect IDs 0 and 7. The
    manager's fields are contiguous from +0x380 in declaration order (HitFX +0x380, SeizeFX +0x3B8,
    SparkFX +0x3F0, from the reflection registration at 0x14059A...), which suggests ID 7 = SeizeFX
    (a throw); the ID -> field consumer is not located, so this stays unproven.
  0x1407911C2 (preset 3) after common effect ID 14 (by the same layout, SparkFX).
  0x1406DEF4B (preset 6): on entering battle state 4 (state machine 0x1406E2530, jump table
    0x1406E2788), after choosing camera mode 7 or 8 at random. State 4's handler counts a timer down
    each tick; whether it is the fight or the knockout state is not settled.
None of the native paths runs on ordinary hits.

LABELED HIT SHAKE (remaster choice, 27 Oct 2026, user request)
VersusGame > Hit Shake (on by default): a clean hit plays original preset 3 (12 frames, 0.70 deg
pitch nod) and a counter hit preset 4 (18 frames, 1.05 deg). Not original behaviour: the original
never shakes on ordinary hits. A move-data shake always takes precedence (a hit never replaces a
running data shake; a data shake replaces a hit shake). Blocked hits do not shake. Untick to get the
faithful camera.

0x84CC IS A FOLLOW-RATE REQUEST, NOT A LAUNCH ZOOM (recovered 28 Oct 2026)
The remaster used to pull the camera in to 84% for 0.45 s on every 0x84CC ("preset zoom on airborne
reactions"). That was a guess and is removed. Tools/CameraEffects/export_follow_rates.py reads the
inspected executable and writes catalog.json "followRate":
  interpreter case 0x140719875 (small jump table 0x71FC70, cases 0x84C3-0x84CC) stores the value's low
    16 bits as the fighter's camera request (+0x36C). It stays until the next 0x84CC or a throw
    camera (0x140722320, from the moveset's throw_cameras block) replaces it, and is cleared only at
    round start (fighter init 0x14074AFD0).
  fight camera view update 0x1406EE020 (vtable 0x1428BBF50 slot 0) looks both requests up in a
    7-entry table and takes the largest rate: 18, 19 -> x1; 11 -> x3; 12 -> x2; 13 -> x1.5;
    14 -> x0.5; 15 -> x0.25 of the base rate 0.1 (settings defaults 0x140740700). No match -> base.
    The view's rate steps toward that by +0.003 / -0.002 per frame, and each frame the camera moves
    that fraction of the way to its framing goal (0x1406E7300). Nothing zooms.
  Data: walks and sidesteps set 18, air reactions (sDw_AIR00_, aDw_*) 0, stomps 16, throw escapes
    0x8FD, item moves 3; Kazuya's Kz_Rider 11, Jin's Jz_shoryu24 / Jz_rplp01 12, Xiaoyu's rolls and
    throw moves 13.
Versus: OnMoveProperty keeps each fighter's request; AfterTick steps the rate once per battle tick;
the camera's three smoothing times (yaw .38, distance .32, target .2 s, the remaster's tuning at the
base rate) are divided by rate / base. CPU matches (harness FollowRate): the old zoom fired 0-3 times
a round; the original rate leaves its base after Jz_rplp01 (x2) or Fu_RrollA (x1.5) and stays there
until a walk, sidestep or air reaction replaces the request, or the round ends.
Not wired: throw cameras' requests; requests 1-6 and 25-41, which also start a special camera
(0x1406E7B00) in battle states 4/21/22; keeping 0x8FD while fighter +0x314 is set. The remaster's
spring smoothing is not the original's per-frame lerp; only the rate changes are original.

KO CAMERA AND KO SLOW-DOWN, RECOVERED FROM THE EXECUTABLE (28 Oct 2026)
The remaster's KO used to be a guess: 30% slow motion for 1.2 s (x1.6 on the final round), the rig pulled in to
72% and re-centred on the loser. Removed. Tools/CameraEffects/export_ko_camera.py reads the inspected executable
and writes catalog.json "koCamera". The fight state (0x1406DE720, state 4 of 0x1406E2530), on a KO:
  slow table entry 2 = {speed 1, 90 frames} (0x140740A30; 150 frames when the winner's Rage Drive is active). The
    table is ticked once per frame (0x140741F20) and each fighter's advance is multiplied by speed / 100
    (0x140758790): a near freeze for 1.5 s. The reference match agrees: at 146.0 s (TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23
    08-04-08.mp4) the action stands almost still under the K.O. call until 147.47 s, then the fall plays at speed.
  camera mode 7 (0x140700B50, update 0x1406EF580): a copy of the view shown at the KO with its FOV replaced by 35,
    weighted over the live view: frame/5 for 5 frames, 1 until frame 95, then back over 20 frames (frame 115).
    The FOV is horizontal degrees: a read-only capture of the running game saw 65 normally and 35 in 8 of 120
    samples (Tools/EntranceCamera/gameplay-camera-capture.json). The view does not follow anyone; it is held.
  shake preset 6 (0x1406DEF4B; 42 frames, 2.11 deg). This settles the earlier open question of which native path
    plays a large nod at a KO. The held KO view covers it while the hold lasts.
  Rage Drive: move property 0x801B (Jin's Jz_shoryu_EX*, Kazuya's Kz_dslp_EX / Kz_dsrp00n_EX, Xiaoyu's Fu_R_clk*_EX,
    Alisa's Aa_66RP*_EX, Katarina's Ln_6rkrp_*) sets fighter +0xC02 (0x140719CBA); a KO while the winner has it sets
    game +0x260 (0x1406EA292), which picks the 150-frame slow-down and camera mode 8 instead of mode 7.
Versus: AfterTick starts it when the round ends by KO (not time-up): Time.timeScale = 0.01, shake preset 6, and
the last shown view (without shake) becomes the KO view. Camera effects now step once per 60 Hz frame of real
time (VersusGame.AdvanceCameraFrames), as the original camera keeps updating through the freeze: the KO camera
and its 115 frames, the near-freeze's 90/150 frames, and also every shake and the follow rate (both used to step
per battle tick). The KO view is blended over the live view (position lerp, rotation slerp, FOV lerp) by the
mode 7 weights. The match winner's victory take now starts 1.2 s of full speed after the freeze (2.7 s after the
KO; was 2.4 s), so the death fall still plays first.
Harness KoCamera (CPU matches, 6 pairings): 13 KOs, none by Rage Drive (the CPU never starts one).
Not wired: no KO camera after a throw KO (winner +0x314 set, unless Rage Drive) or when either fighter has +0x490 & 2; the
battle model has no throw state. The KO's alternative mode 7 call when game +0x22C is set (FOV 39, no hold).

RAGE DRIVE KO SHOT (camera mode 8, recovered 28 Oct 2026)
A KO while the winner's Rage Drive (0x801B) is active plays camera mode 8 instead of the mode 7 close-up, with the
150-frame near-freeze. export_ko_camera.py writes it as koCamera.rageDrive. The fight state picks variant 0 or 1 at
random (LCG 0x1406E3720) and calls 0x1407008A0 -> 0x140700580; the update is 0x1406EF2C0 -> 0x1406F3BF0:
  no blend: the shot replaces the live view for 150 frames, then the camera cuts back.
  centre fighter C: P1, or on the match-deciding KO the match winner (game +0x268, written by 0x1406EBCE4 from the
    rounds won); variant 1 centres on C's opponent instead (fighter +0x1548).
  each frame, s = sin(80 deg * t) / sin(80 deg), t = frame / 150: FOV 20 -> 35 (horizontal), angle -30 -> -60 deg
    (variant 0) or -60 -> -45 deg (variant 1), times the side sign (-1 when C's +0x14D4 is 1). The camera sits on
    the floor line from C to its opponent, rotated by that angle about the vertical, (|C->opponent| + 4000 mm) from
    C and 100 mm above C's base, looking at the live fight camera's target (view 1 +0x10); roll = sign * 15 deg.
    Units are millimetres with y up (the fight camera's own distances are ~3400 mm).
Versus: VersusGame picks the variant and centre at the KO and BattleCameraEffects.RageDriveKoShot poses the camera
from the shown fighters, the live view's position and the spring rig's target. Interpretation, not yet compared with
footage: the original's right-handed rotation with the side sign is taken to put the camera on the viewer's side of
the fight line, so the swing goes toward the live camera; the roll direction follows the same side sign but its
handedness is unverified. Framing check (scratch replica, fighters 1.2-2.5 m apart): a low telephoto, about 5-6 m
from the target, framing both fighters from the waist up and widening as the FOV opens. The CPU never starts a Rage
Drive, so try it in manual play (Jin's Jz_shoryu_EX, Kazuya's Kz_dslp_EX, Xiaoyu's Fu_R_clk00_EX in Rage).

TRADE SLOW-DOWN ("close call"), RECOVERED FROM THE EXECUTABLE (28 Oct 2026)
The both-fighter event in hit resolution 0x140713DD0 (noted above under the native shake requests) is a trade: on
a frame where each fighter took a hit (+0x7CC, the damage taken, > 0, struck flag +0x754 & 0x20; or both fighters
+0x754 & 0x220; a fighter in a throw, +0x314 < 0, cancels it) and either took at least 17 damage, the game sets
slow table entry 2 = {10%, 24 frames} (0x140714A12), shake preset 2 + level 3 = 5 (0x140714B78), and camera mode 7
holding the shown view at FOV 35, 4 frames in, 20 held, 30 out (0x140714BE1). Settings 0x1434DF5D0..0x1434DF5EC,
from the same static initializer. The KO later in the frame writes the same slow entry and mode 7, so a trade that
ends the round plays as a KO. export_ko_camera.py writes it as catalog.json "tradeCamera".
Versus: OnHit records the damage of each clean hit per tick; AfterTick starts the slow-down when both fighters took
one on the same tick (battle still in Fight) and the larger is >= 17, through the same StartHold path as the KO
(VersusGame.holdFrame / slowPercent / HoldWeight). Interpretation: the struck flag is taken as "clean, not
blocked"; the throw cancel is not wired (the model has no throw state). Harness Trade (30 CPU matches, 70 rounds):
13 trades, 9 slow-downs (e.g. Jz_shoryu24 25 dmg vs Fu_lk00F, Kz_tukirpsp 25 vs Ln_lp00) - about one per 8 rounds.
Not checked against footage.

TRADE TIMING, REMASTER TUNING (user request 28 Oct 2026, not original)
VersusGame > Trade Length (default 2.5, 1 = original) stretches the trade's slow-down and close-up, and Smooth
Trade (default on) eases them: game speed glides from 100% to 10% over the close-up's blend-in (doubled when smooth)
and back over its blend-out, and the close-up weight follows a SmoothStep. Defaults: 10% held to frame 60, speed back
by frame 135; close-up 20 frames in, held to frame 70, out by frame 145 (about 2.4 s in all). The original switches
speed at once after 24 frames and blends linearly over 4 / 20 / 30 frames: Trade Length 1 with Smooth Trade off. The
TRADE EFFECT button (F5) uses the same settings. The KO keeps the original timing.
Centre Trade (default on, user request): the close-up stays at the held position but turns each frame to the centre
of both fighters' framing bones (head, hands, feet, hips) and widens past FOV 35 only as far as needed to fit them
inside the same margins as the fight camera (VersusGame.CentreOnFighters). The original holds a fixed view.

BATTLE EFFECT TUNING SCENE (user request 28 Oct 2026)
Every remaster choice on top of the original effect data now lives in one asset,
Assets/_Project/Resources/BattleEffectTuning.asset (Scripts/Runtime/BattleEffectTuning.cs), read live by every
VersusGame: common effects on/off, hit effect size and glare, the hit shake (on/off, presets, strength of all
shakes), follow-rate influence and the camera's three smoothing times, the trade (threshold, speed, length,
FOV, ease, centring) and the KO (speed, length, FOV, ease, centring, Rage Drive shot). The per-scene VersusGame
fields it replaces (commonBattleEffects, hitShake, tradeLength, smoothTrade, centreTrade) are gone;
Temp/localversus2-common-effects now writes the asset.
Scene Assets/_Project/Scenes/BattleEffectTuning.unity (Remaster > Battle Effects > Create tuning scene, or flag
Temp/create-effect-tuning-scene; Scripts/Editor/BattleEffectTuningSetup.cs) is a copy of the saved LocalVersus2
that skips character select, auto-fights and shows the tuning panel (VersusGame.effectTuningPanel; F6 toggles the
panel in any battle). The panel (Scripts/Runtime/VersusGameTuningPanel.cs) has sliders for every value, buttons
that play each effect on demand without changing the fight (HIT / COUNTER / GUARD at the struck point, the three
shakes, TRADE, KO, RAGE DRIVE KO), SAVE (writes the asset; in a player build, a JSON next to the save data),
ORIGINAL (the inspected executable's behaviour: no hit shake, original timing, fixed views) and DEFAULTS (the
remaster's first tuning). Values change live; only SAVE keeps them for other battles and across Play sessions.
Exchanges (user request, same day): in the tuning scene the fighters stand idle (VersusGame.effectDemosOnly, a
panel toggle) and the HIT / COUNTER / GUARD / TRADE / KO / RAGE DRIVE KO buttons make them act the exchange out
(Scripts/Runtime/BattleEffectDemo.cs): both are placed on the fight line at a measured distance, then P1 presses the
button whose move lands fastest (a jab); for COUNTER P2 starts its slowest landing press first so the jab lands in
its startup; for GUARD P2 holds back (the distance is the one its guard was measured at, since holding back walks
it away); for TRADE both press their heaviest landing move, the slower first, so both land on one tick; KO puts P2
at 1 health; RAGE DRIVE KO also grants P1 Rage and forces its 0x801B move (the movelist demos carry no Rage Drive
input). Which press is which move, its startup, reach and whether a standing guard blocks it are measured on a
scratch BattleModel with the same movesets each time, so new fighters need no table. Press again to replay; a KO
replays after the round restarts. Harness EffectDemo: 30 of 30 exchanges (5 pairings) produce their event. The
presentation-only buttons (HIT FX, shakes, the three cameras) remain below them.

THE LOSER'S KO ACTION STARTS AT ONCE (user request 28 Oct 2026, remaster choice)
Measured (harness KoAction, 55 CPU KOs at full speed): a KO'ing blow on a standing fighter played its whole stagger
(sDm_tohtai 37 ticks, sDm_dm_spin_r 43), returned the fighter to its stance for a tick, and only then did the
stance's "on death" cancel (condition 135) start fatal_s, the 80-tick standing collapse: death move at tick 31
(26-44), lying at 111. Launched KOs were already falling within 2 ticks. On top of that the KO near-freeze held
everyone still for 1.5 s.
Now (BattleEffectTuning, both on by default; ORIGINAL turns them off):
  Standing loser collapses at once (koCollapseAtOnce -> BattleModel.immediateKoCollapse): a KO'ing, unblocked blow
    whose reaction keeps the body on its feet (never airborne, not downed) forces the loser's fatal_s on the blow's
    tick. KoAction: 29 of 55 KOs, death move at tick 0, lying at 80. Launched KOs unchanged.
  Slow-down holds only the winner (koFreezeWinnerOnly): the KO's 1% slow-down is applied to the winner in the model
    (BattleModel.SlowFighter, the 0x828C mechanism) instead of Time.timeScale, so the loser's KO action plays at
    full speed under the KO camera.
Whether the original skips the stagger is not established from the executable: the move data's route is the
stagger -> stance -> fatal_s one. Baselines after the change: KoStand 1 of 116 (the known sDw_JACKup case),
KoOverlap slide median 1.26 m (was 1.45), bodies close 0.3% of ticks (unchanged), Jitter 0.9 reversals.
