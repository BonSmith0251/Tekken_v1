BATTLE HUD: LIFE GAUGE EFFECTS (LocalVersus / LocalVersus2)

The life gauge's behaviour is ported from the original Scaleform movie, not estimated from video.
SW_UI_HUD_Gauge.uasset holds an uncompressed GFX (SWF 12) movie at 60 fps; its sprite timelines
(frame labels, per-frame placements, colour transforms, blend modes) and its ActionScript 3 (Main:
SetHealth, StartGaugeAnim, StopGaugeAnim, onRedScale, onDamage, RageOn/RageOff/RageEnd) were decoded
with Tools/BattleHud/gfx_dump.py and abc_dis.py. BattleHud.cs runs the same logic on a 60 Hz clock.

What the original does, and what the remaster now draws
- Hit (SetHealth, health fell): the green cuts at once; the red damage stays where it was.
- Gauge shake (onDamage): the whole gauge jumps to alternate sides, starting at 15 x 5 movie pixels;
  each jump is perFrame/10 smaller, jumps come every 3 frames, then every 2 (after 4 frames), then
  every frame (after 7 frames). It fades out over about 40 frames.
- Damage frame (HUD_GAU_LIFE_FRAME_DAMAGE_Anim): two copies of the gold damage frame rise for two
  frames and hold (0.33 / 0.6) until the shake's third stage, then peak (frame 3 / frame 7) and fade
  out by frame 14.
- Hit glow (GAU_Life_eff_sub): eff_damage on the cut, 49 frames: it widens 25% -> 78% in 8 frames and
  narrows back, at 20-40% alpha, sheared with the gauge's lean. It ends early when the red finishes.
- Red trail (StartGaugeAnim + onRedScale): the game holds it natively (StopGaugeAnim/StartGaugeAnim).
  The remaster holds it while the struck fighter is still in a hit reaction, which matches the
  reference: a single hit held ~28 frames in TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23 08-04-08.mp4 (75.30 s); the reaction
  rule gives a median of 30 (HudTrail harness). It then slides linearly to the new level over 24
  frames whatever the amount (DENOMINATOR_OF_SPEED is set natively; measured 11.5 frames at 30 fps).
- Red glow (GAU_Life_redgrow, 35%): a thinner red strip under the red that follows the draining edge
  at 85% of its speed (GLOW_DELAY_MAGNIFICATION), so a faint red tail lags behind.
- Rage (GAU_Life_Frame 'Rage'): the normal frame stays and the red rage outline pulses over it,
  0 -> 1 -> 0 over 18 frames, with a second copy at 10-30%. (Before this change the gauge swapped to
  the rage outline, which has no dark interior.)
- New round: red and glow snap back to full.

Approximations
- The native hold (StopGaugeAnim/StartGaugeAnim) is replaced by the hit reaction; after knockdowns the
  red can hold up to about 3 s, until the fighter recovers.
- Additive layers: the red's second copy (+20%, +49% as a drain starts), the hit glow and the faint
  rage outline copy are additive in the movie. IMGUI only alpha-blends, so the red is drawn at its
  effective 96% opacity and the other two alpha-blended.
- The damage frame's filter (Scaleform filter on one copy) is not reproduced.
- Not used: Rage_End (Rage is never spent in the battle model) and Focus_Start (a cyan zoom-in of the
  frame whose trigger is native and unknown). The back plates (HUD_GAU_LIFE_BACK_*_000) are empty in
  the default skin.

Regenerate
  python Tools/BattleHud/export_battle_hud.py --game <TEKKEN.7> --t7extract <asset_workshop> --work <scratch>
  (adds LifeGrow.png = GAU_Life_redgrow)
Inspect the movie
  python -m t7extract unpack <game> "*/flash/SW_UI_HUD_Gauge/SW_UI_HUD_Gauge.uasset" -o <scratch>
  python Tools/BattleHud/gfx_dump.py <scratch>/.../SW_UI_HUD_Gauge.uasset > gauge.json
  python Tools/BattleHud/abc_dis.py "<scratch>/**/SW_UI_HUD_Gauge.uasset" "Main\.(SetHealth|onDamage|onRedScale)"

ROUND CALLS: READY, FIGHT, K.O., DOUBLE K.O. (2026-10-27)

These replay the original SW_UI_GameMessage timelines instead of a generic pop-in.
- Tools/BattleHud/export_game_message.py decodes the movie (gfx_dump.py, now with clip depths,
  PlaceObject3 blend modes, GFX external images and shape bounds) and writes
  Resources/BattleHud/GameMessage.json plus the 49 images the call clips place (GameMessage/*.png).
- Scripts/Runtime/GfxClip.cs replays a clip: Flash goto rebuilds, nested sprites, matrices (with skew),
  multiply/add colour transforms; named *_mc clips hold on their last frame (their frame scripts call
  stop()), unnamed ones loop.
- READY = ReadyFight_mc 'Ready' (frames 1-39, then holds); FIGHT = 'Fight' (40-98), and it keeps
  playing into the first ~0.25 s of the fight; K.O. = KO_mc (1-120); DOUBLE K.O. = WKO_mc.
- Round 2+ intros now run ROUND (60 ticks), READY (45), FIGHT (45), as in the original; before, READY
  only showed during the match entrance.
Rendering (2026-10-27, second pass, from the movie data only): each image is drawn with
Resources/BattleHud/GfxLayer.shader through Graphics.DrawTexture: the Flash colour transform (multiply
+ add on gamma-space colour), additive blend mode 8, the BlurFilter (box blur; motion blur on the
fly-ins) and clip-layer masks. The masks are vector letter outlines (READY, FIGHT, K.O., DOUBLE),
rasterised by the exporter to GameMessage/Mask_<id>.png, so the highlight sweeps shine inside the
letters only. If the shader is missing, GfxClip falls back to GUI.DrawTexture (no add/mask/blur).
ROUND, TIME UP, DRAW and PERFECT still use the older pop-in (Round_mc, TimeUp_mc, Draw_mc and
Perfect_mc are exported and ready for GfxClip).
Checked with a Python port of the player rendering from the data (Scratch: gfxmock2.py); the user
asked to work from the game data rather than the videos. Not yet judged in a match.

ROUND CALLS, THIRD PASS (2026-10-27): the fly-ins are 3D motion tweens
The "scaling flying in" lives in ActionScript, not in the timeline placements:
- Each call clip's constructor builds Flash IDE motion tweens (fl.motion MotionBase segments with per-frame
  x/y/z, rotationX/Y/Z, scale/skew, colour, visibility, blend and blur arrays, plus an initialPosition, e.g.
  ROUND starts at z -1200 and swings rotationY 39 -> -20). Tools/BattleHud/motion_dump.py extracts them from
  abc_dis.py's disassembly; export_game_message.py writes them into GameMessage.json ("motions").
- GfxClip ports the movie's own Animator3D.setTime3D: Initial * T(initialPosition) * T(x,y,z) * Rz*Ry*Rx *
  ScaleSkew (Initial = the target's placement when it appeared), projected with Main.__setPerspectiveProjection_
  (fieldOfView 60, centre 960,540) as a homogeneous GUI.matrix.
- PlaceObject3 visibility (HasVisible) is honoured: most layers are placed hidden and revealed on cue.
- Looping clips keep their persisting instances (Flash semantics), which keeps the ROUND digit script.
- Round_mc.ChangeRound(n) is ported (BattleHud.RoundClip), so ROUND n, FINAL ROUND, TIME UP, DRAW and
  PERFECT now also replay the movie.
Verified with a Python port of the same player (scratch gfxmock3.py) from the data alone.
