BATTLE EFFECTS (LocalVersus / LocalVersus2)

ON IN LOCALVERSUS2 (27 Oct 2026, user request): the common set below plays alongside each fighter's
own wired effects (CHARACTER_BATTLE_EFFECTS.txt). It was off earlier that day. The switch is
VersusGame > Common Battle Effects (C# default off; LocalVersus2 stores it on; ticking or unticking it
during Play takes effect at once; round restarts clear common effects still playing). Flag
Temp/localversus2-common-effects ("on"/"off") saves it in the scene. The trigger rules below (Pierce
threshold, impact point, Rage) are remaster choices, not recovered native dispatch.

The fight plays Tekken 7's own common battle effects. The original keeps them in
Effect/TK75/Common; BP_CommonEffectManager names the particle system for each role (BP_EF_KO the
knockout one), and the game's native code decides when each plays. The remaster triggers them from
its own events, matched against the reference captures (Videos/Captures/tk7, TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23):

  Clean hit                 HitFX           PS_EF_cmnhit00_ts       orange glow and spark spray
  Counter hit               CriticalHit3FX  PS_EF_cmncritical00_ts  orange rings, spark burst, lines
  Guarded hit               GuardFX         PS_EF_cmnguard01_ts     white flash and spark spray
  Rage begins (25% health)  RageFX          PS_EF_cmnragestart00_ss red rings and ribbons around the body
  While in Rage             red rim glow    RageRim.shader          + RageAuraFX's sparse red wisps (***)
  Power crush absorbs hit   Armor0FX        PS_EF_cmnpowerclash00   white aura flash (bloom), in place of the hit spark

  Not played in versus (exported, previews only; audit of 27 Oct 2026, see below):
  Punish (hit in recovery)  PunishNotice    PS_EF_PunishNotice_*    practice-mode crosshair (**)
  Clean hit, 20+ damage     PierceFX        PS_EF_cmnhit_line00     the >= 20 damage trigger was a guess
  Knockout blow             KOFX            PS_EF_SpecialKO         none of 9 reference KOs shows it
  Body lands from the air   BlastFX         PS_EF_cmndown00_ts      no floor ring on solid-floor landings
  Any fall to the floor     SmokeFX         PS_EF_cmnsmoke00        no dust on solid-floor knockdowns

Audit (27 Oct 2026, user: "find the wrong effects like the punish"). Each effect was compared
with frame sequences of TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23 08-04-08.mp4: hits 46/49.6/57.0 s and Kazumi/Heihachi
320/431/628 s (the orange fire spray is the common hit, on every attacker); counter 54.9 s;
guard 135.7 s; Rage start 57.2 s and 233.6 s; Rage 58-62 s; KOs 62, 83, 146, 178, 237, 264, 320,
374, 431, 456 s (hit/counter effect under slow motion only); air landing 236 s and knockdown 218 s
on a solid floor (no ring, no dust; the first round's water stage splashes on its own). The
blue ring with lens-flare needles at 54.9 s is Hwoarang's character effect, not a common one.
Still weaker than the original: the clean hit's spray is smaller and shorter-lived than the
original's ~0.5 m fire spray that drifts for ~0.4 s (vector fields are approximated).

(***) Rage in the original outlines the fighter with a saturated red rim (Devil Jin 58-62 s: rim
pixels median sRGB (192, 0, 16)). RageRim.cs draws a copy of each skinned mesh with the additive
fresnel RageRim shader (power 3.5, intensity 2.2, pushed 4 mm out) while Rage lasts; fitted, the
original's Rage material is not recovered. Capture: Validation/HitEffects/RageRim-front/side.png.
(**) No punish marker in versus (27 Oct 2026, user report "doesn't look like the original"). The blue
ring with spinning lens-flare needles in TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23 08-04-08.mp4 (54.9-55.5 s) is
Hwoarang's own character effect PS_EF_CHR_HWO_nomal (effect ID 0, textures T_EF_COL_hwo_light00_thk
and T_EF_COL_hwo_line01_tss plus the SM_EF_hwo_ring_00 orbit ring, 0.5 s), dispatched by 0x802E on his
kicks (hwomot.bin: 134 rows in 49 moves, e.g. Hw_spkA frame 25 on L_Foot). It was mistaken for
PS_EF_PunishNotice_*, which is the practice-mode crosshair (dashed arcs, triangles, centre dot).
VersusGame no longer plays PracticePunishNotice*; HitEvent.punish stays in the battle model.
Tekken 7 counts a hit during the opponent's startup or active frames as a counter hit and a hit in
the recovery as a normal hit; the battle model follows that (BattleModel.CommandContact).
The model does not spend Rage (no Rage Art consumption), so the aura stays while health is at a
quarter or less, and ends with the round. Both Rage effects travel with the fighter's spine.

Where the effect plays: the data has no hitbox positions for effects, so the struck point is where
the attacker's nearest striking bone (hand, elbow, knee, foot, head) meets the defender's hip-to-head
line, and the effect faces along the blow. The knockout flash plays where the last blow landed.
Effects run on game time, so they slow with the KO.

Conversion (Tools/HitEffects/export_hit_effects.py): every emitter's spawn bursts and rate, lifetime,
size and size-over-life, colour and colour-over-life (HDR peak -> material intensity), velocity,
cone, drag, acceleration, spawn shape, rotation, mesh particles, camera offset, floor placement,
point-light flashes and character-bone spawning are decoded from the original lookup tables and
curves (centimetres -> metres, UE axes -> Unity). Cascade sprite sizes are full sizes: the 50-unit
punish reticle is about half a metre in the reference video. Size axes the editor locked are stored
as zeros and follow X.

Approximated (not recovered):
- Colour space: the exported colours are linear (UE), and Unity decodes particle, gradient, light
  and material colours as sRGB in this linear project, so CascadeEffects.Linear() gamma-encodes
  them first. Before 2026-10-26 every hue came out over-saturated: the counter rings'
  (1, .33, .08) drew as (1, .09, .01), red.
- Brightness: the original multiplies effect colour by a glare power (1..100) and brings the result
  back with its exposure and tonemap. Here the material intensity is the colour peak (peaks above 4
  compressed to 4 * (peak/4)^0.25, so the rings' 60 becomes ~7.9) times the square root of the glare,
  and the particle shader applies a per-channel shoulder (linear to .35, then exponential to 1):
  hot orange turns yellow instead of clipping to white, and the output stays within 1, so the
  default HDRP bloom (threshold 0) cannot blow it out. Fitted to TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23 08-04-08.mp4:
  counter-ring pixels median (226, 177, 110) there, (234, 162, 89) in the capture.
  CascadeEffects.glareGain scales it. The same shader and MaterialFor serve StageEffects.
- GPU vector fields: their tightness becomes drag and their strength becomes noise, which keeps
  sparks compact around the impact; the field shapes themselves are not decoded.
- AlphaClip: the cooked effect materials keep only parameter names, not their maths. The spark
  material's static "Alpha Clip" sharpens coverage to its core, saturate(a * (1 + c) - c), eased off
  for sprites under ~6 pixels across (none at 3; the 1-1.5 cm hit sparks vanished otherwise); the
  distortion material's per-particle AlphaClip curve erodes it, a ^ max(1, c). Both were fitted
  against the captures.
- The practice punish crosshair (preview only): the stripped crosshair material assembles it from
  quarter textures, so the capture draws the complete glyph in its glare texture.
- KO flash lines are widened from 4 mm to about 3 cm; KO dust is capped at 600 fragments/s and 60
  smoke sprites/s (the original spawns 3,000 and 1,500).
Not reproduced: texture scroll/distortion dynamic parameters, refraction-only emitters, GPU floor
collision of KO debris, and bursts above 600 particles (capped).
