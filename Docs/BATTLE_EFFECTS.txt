BATTLE EFFECTS (LocalVersus / LocalVersus2)

The fight plays Tekken 7's own common battle effects. The original keeps them in
Effect/TK75/Common; BP_CommonEffectManager names the particle system for each role (BP_EF_KO the
knockout one), and the game's native code decides when each plays. The remaster triggers them from
its own events, matched against the reference captures (Videos/Captures, TEKKEN 7 2026-10-23):

  Clean hit                 HitFX           PS_EF_cmnhit00_ts       small orange glow and sparks
  Counter hit               CriticalHit3FX  PS_EF_cmncritical00_ts  orange rings, spark burst, lines
  Punish (hit in recovery)  PunishNotice    PS_EF_PunishNotice_*    blue-white reticle, by hit level (**)
  Guarded hit               GuardFX         PS_EF_cmnguard01_ts     guard flash, lines, smoke puff
  Clean hit, 20+ damage     PierceFX        PS_EF_cmnhit_line00     directional speed lines (*)
  Knockout blow             KOFX            PS_EF_SpecialKO         radial flash lines, powder, floor ring, dust
  Body lands from the air   BlastFX         PS_EF_cmndown00_ts      floor rings and wind circle
  Any fall to the floor     SmokeFX         PS_EF_cmnsmoke00        dust
  Rage begins (25% health)  RageFX          PS_EF_cmnragestart00_ss rings and ribbons around the body
  While in Rage             RageAuraFX      PS_EF_cmnrageaura_ts    looping red aura on the body bones

(*) The original chooses PierceFX in native code; using it for heavy hits is this project's choice.
(**) The reference matches show the practice-mode punish reticle in normal play, on hits that land
while the opponent recovers from an attack. Tekken 7 counts a hit during the opponent's startup or
active frames as a counter hit and a hit in the recovery as a normal hit (the punish); the battle
model follows that (BattleModel.CommandContact). Its colour follows the reference video (light
blue); the data tints the mid variant yellow-white, which the stripped material may have changed.
The model does not spend Rage (no Rage Art consumption), so the aura stays while health is at a
quarter or less, and ends with the round. Both Rage effects travel with the fighter's spine.

Where the effect plays: the data has no hitbox positions for effects, so the struck point is where
the attacker's nearest striking bone (hand, elbow, knee, foot, head) meets the defender's hip-to-head
line, and the effect faces along the blow. The knockout flash plays where the last blow landed.
Effects run on game time, so they slow with the KO.

Conversion (Tools/HitEffects/export_hit_effects.py): every emitter's spawn bursts and rate, lifetime,
size and size-over-life, colour and colour-over-life (HDR peak -> material intensity), velocity,
cone, drag, acceleration, spawn shape, rotation, mesh particles, camera offset, floor placement,
point-light flashes and character-bone spawning are decoded from the original lookup tables and
curves (centimetres -> metres, UE axes -> Unity). Cascade sprite sizes are full sizes: the 50-unit
punish reticle is about half a metre in the reference video. Size axes the editor locked are stored
as zeros and follow X.

Approximated (not recovered):
- Brightness: the original multiplies effect colour by a glare power (1..100) and lets bloom and the
  tonemap whiten the core. The versus scene has neither, so the material intensity is the colour
  peak times the square root of the glare, and the particle shader whitens hot colour itself
  (85% toward white by 9x). CascadeEffects.glareGain scales it.
- GPU vector fields: their tightness becomes drag and their strength becomes noise, which keeps
  sparks compact around the impact; the field shapes themselves are not decoded.
- AlphaClip: the cooked effect materials keep only parameter names, not their maths. The spark
  material's static "Alpha Clip" sharpens coverage to its core, saturate(a * (1 + c) - c); the
  distortion material's per-particle AlphaClip curve erodes it, a ^ max(1, c). Both were fitted
  against the captures.
- The punish reticle: the stripped crosshair material assembles it from quarter textures, so the
  complete glyph in its glare texture is drawn instead, tinted the light blue of the reference video.
- KO flash lines are widened from 4 mm to about 3 cm; KO dust is capped at 600 fragments/s and 60
  smoke sprites/s (the original spawns 3,000 and 1,500).
Not reproduced: texture scroll/distortion dynamic parameters, refraction-only emitters, GPU floor
collision of KO debris, and bursts above 600 particles (capped).

Regenerate:
  python -m t7extract unpack <game> "*/Effect/TK75/Common/*" -o <src>
  python -m t7extract unpack <game> "*/Effect/TK75/Character/mrx/Mesh/SM_EF_MRX_*" -o <src>
  python Tools/HitEffects/export_hit_effects.py --source <src> --t7extract <asset_workshop>
Output: Assets/_Project/Resources/HitEffects (effects.json, Textures, Meshes, CascadeParticle.shader).

Preview: Remaster > Rendering > Capture battle effects writes Validation/HitEffects/<role>-<ms>.png.
The project needs the built-in Particle System module (com.unity.modules.particlesystem).
